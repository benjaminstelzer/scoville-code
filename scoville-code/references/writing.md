# Shared writing rules

These rules cover plans, decisions, instructions, agent messages, handoffs,
reviews and reports. Where a schema, template or delivery contract fixes the
shape, keep that shape, because its consumer depends on it.

## Read what you rely on

Read in full every text you rely on, change or judge: instructions, the
original assignment, an agent's final message, binding Plan or Decision text
and source passages.

- Fetch the missing part of a truncated or partial result before the work that
  depends on it, or say what you could not read.
- A read the tool reports as complete needs no second read.
- Read as much as the next decision needs and reuse complete results you
  hold. Read more only to settle a concrete open question, not for coverage.

## Evidence-based conclusions

Support a factual or technical conclusion with its deciding evidence, and keep
it when someone objects:

- Name the deciding evidence, for example file and line, command output or a
  quoted passage.
- Check counterevidence in its source, not in a summary.
- When an objection fails, say why.

With or without an objection: when a fact, requirement, assumption or
inference proves wrong, incomplete or superseded, state the correction. State
what remains uncertain or disputed.

Confidence, repetition, authority and agreement are not evidence. Where you
disagree, the user's preferences and authorized decisions still set the scope.

## Write for the recipient

Give the recipient what they need to understand, verify or continue: scope,
results, distinct failures, blockers, limits and the next permitted action.

- Report equivalent results and identical failures once, with what they
  affect. Keep different causes and different effects on acceptance apart.
- Leave out routine chronology, raw logs, passing-check output, unchanged
  inventories and bookkeeping unless they are required or decide something.
- Cite existing detailed evidence; do not copy it or report it twice.
- Choose prose, labelled fields, lists, tables or diagrams by what reads
  clearest.

If you do not know the recipient's context, assume none. State the facts, constraints and authority the next correct action needs; do not rely on chat history. Where they can
read a source, give its exact path, section and what to read it for. Still
state directly what they need before that read and what a delivery contract or evidence contract requires. Quote wording that decides the matter or is required. A summary
line does not replace a needed explanation.

When you condense or forward, copy these unchanged: required facts, the user's
exact wording, schemas, statuses, control messages, identities, permissions,
stops, quiescence and delivery contracts. A paraphrase can change what the
next agent may do. An explicit edit request and required secret redaction
still apply.

- Use the language and format of the current request. Forward quoted
  user-facing text unchanged.
- Answer a question or assessment request; do not act on it. "Is this function
  used?" gets an answer, not a deletion.
- During longer work, an agent that answers the user directly gives brief
  material findings and next actions, then continues authorized work.
- The final report stands on its own. Say whether the whole task is complete,
  give the results of the checks it rests on and name any missing input or
  decision, in plain sentences; leave out a point with nothing to report.

An agent whose role cannot run commands or save files returns its complete
text through its result channel. If that channel cannot carry it, the agent
names the limit and the caller handles delivery.
